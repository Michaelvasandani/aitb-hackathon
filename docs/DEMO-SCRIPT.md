# DEMO SCRIPT — 4 minutes, showcase 16:00 Sun 2 Aug 2026

Frozen at 14:00 with everything else. After 14:00 the wording can change. **The click path
cannot.**

**Demo target:** Fresno, CA · **Saturday 31 October 2026** · room cap 60. A city nobody on this
team has a relationship in. That is the claim.

The date is load-bearing and was checked against `countback.py`. 31 October is 90 days out —
12.9 weeks — which clears the 56-day floor *and* stays above the `WEEKS_OUT < 12` conditional.
24 October is 11.86 weeks and would make the tool print a compression warning while the presenter
says "twelve weeks of dated plan." Do not change this date.

**The payoff beat is beat 3** — ending chunk 2, when the tool hands over a whole twelve-week
timeline in exchange for four answers. Everything before it sets that up. Everything after it is
scrolling down and back up inside the same document, so we never navigate away from the
strongest screen. The timeline is what is on the projector when the clock stops.

## The click path

Total budget 3:45 with a 15-second reserve. Chase gives one silent hand signal at 2:00 and one
at 3:30. No verbal interruptions, ever.

### Beat 1 — Cold open · 0:00–0:25 · 25 seconds

**Screen:** `plan.html`, chunk 1, empty. Nothing typed yet.
**Clicks:** none. Hands off the keyboard.

**Say:**

> "We asked the founder of AI Trailblazers what was hardest about running one of these. He said:
> *the coordination of tasks and the coordination of responsibilities, and then being flexible
> enough to change it when everything changes.*"

> "Nobody on this team has ever been to Fresno. Watch."

*Do not explain the product. Do not say the word "platform." Two sentences, then type.*

---

### Beat 2 — Four questions · 0:25–1:10 · 45 seconds

**Screen:** chunk 1 (DECIDE), then chunk 2 (LOCK).

**Clicks, in order:**

1. Type city: `Fresno, CA`
2. Type the anchor: `Fresno County Public Library`
3. Click **Next** — gate 1 passes, chunk 2 opens
4. Type date: `31 October 2026`
5. Type cap: `60`
6. Click **Next** — gate 2 passes

**Say, over chunk 1:**

> "It asks four things — and it never asks for a venue before you have one, because an organizer
> in week one doesn't have a venue, and asking makes the tool feel like paperwork."

**Say, over chunk 2:**

> "Now it wants the date and the room, because that's the order the real runbook goes in — you
> cannot pitch a sponsor before you have a date and a venue. Those are your proof."

*Type steadily. Do not narrate your own typing. If you fumble a field, keep talking and fix it —
never apologize on stage.*

### Beat 3 — THE PAYOFF · 1:10–1:50 · 40 seconds

**Screen:** the twelve-week dated timeline appears.
**Clicks:** one slow scroll down the timeline, top to bottom. Nothing else.

**Say, as it appears — then stop talking for three full seconds:**

> "Four answers. Twelve weeks of dated plan."

*The three seconds of silence is the single most important instruction in this document. Let the
room read the screen. Do not fill it.*

**Then, over the scroll:**

> "Every date on this screen was computed, not generated — the dependency graph and the countback
> math never touch a model, because an organizer who catches us being wrong about a date stops
> trusting us about venues too."

*Screen reads 90 days. Say "twelve weeks." Both are true, and the screen never contradicts you.*

### Beat 4 — What makes this real · 1:50–2:40 · 50 seconds

**Screen:** the Local leads section — venue, sponsor, in-kind and mentor cards.
**Clicks:** continue scrolling into the leads cards. Then switch to **tab 2** (pre-loaded, no

**Say, over the cards:**

**Say, over tab 2:**

> "That link goes to the actual page — and a separate pass, whose only job is to kill leads,
> checked every one against its source and either confirmed it or dropped it."

**Say, back on tab 1:**

> "Eight real names an organizer can email today beat forty plausible ones. One invented venue
> would end this product, so we built the thing that stops it."

## Failure plan

Rehearse each of these once. A fallback nobody has run is not a fallback.

### A. The live URL is down

**Do not debug on stage. Do not narrate the failure.**

Michael presses Cmd+O and opens the local copy from disk. The artifact is one self-contained HTML
file with no external requests — that architecture choice exists precisely for this moment. The
script does not change; the demo continues from beat 1.

- **Owner:** Michael.
- **Precondition, 15:45:** the local copy has been opened once with wifi off and confirmed
  identical to live. Ruffa signs off in channel.
- Beat 4 already uses a pre-loaded tab, so no beat in this script requires the network.

### B. Research came back thin

**Do not pad and do not skip beat 4.** Run beat 4 on whatever survived verification, and lead with
the number:

> "The tool found three venues in Fresno, and it says so on the screen."

Then move straight into beat 5 and spend the borrowed ten seconds there. A short verified list
demonstrates the verification layer better than a long one does, and honesty is the claim we are
making. Every judge in the room has seen a demo pad a list.

- **Owner:** Van.
- **Precondition, 14:00:** `leads.json` freezes. Whatever it contains at 14:00 is what we demo.
  No research after freeze, for any reason, including "it'll only take a second."

### C. The laptop dies, the projector refuses, or the browser wipes state

**Fallback 1 — the recording. About 30 seconds to recover.**
A 3:30 screen capture with audio, recorded during rehearsal 2 at 15:00. Lives in three places:
Chase's phone, a USB stick in Chase's pocket, the team Drive folder. Chase queues it and hands
over the phone or the stick; Michael narrates live over the top of it. Recorded, not hypothetical.

- **Owner:** Chase. Recorded at 15:00. If it is not recorded by 15:20, that is an escalation.

**Fallback 2 — paper. Zero seconds to recover, always works.**
Ruffa prints at 14:30: the artifact printed to PDF (header, your-answers, the timeline, four lead
cards with source URLs printed as readable text, and the warnings block), plus the six contingency
cards one per page. Six copies, in a folder, on the table before 15:55. If the screen is gone
entirely, Michael hands pages to the front row and walks the same six beats off paper in the same
order. Organizers print things and put them on a check-in table — the artifact was built to
survive this.

- **Owner:** Ruffa. Printed by 14:30.

### D. The clock is slipping

If beat 3 has not happened by **1:30**, cut beat 5 entirely and go 4 → 6. Do not compress beat 3;
it is the only beat that cannot be rushed. Chase signals at 2:00 and 3:30 with a raised hand and
says nothing.

### E. Someone asks a question mid-demo

> "Hold that — I'll answer it in thirty seconds when I finish."

Never answer during the four minutes. The reserve is not for questions.
